Global patterns of climate change impacts on desert bird communities

Ma L.*, Conradie S. R., Crawford C. L., Gardner A. S., Kearney M. R., Maclean I. M. D., McKechnie A. E., Mi C.-R., Senior R. A., Wilcove D. S.

Nature Communications 14, 211 (2023) · Read on nature.com · DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-35814-8

Warm deserts are projected to warm disproportionately, yet global desert biodiversity impacts remain poorly resolved. Birds there already live near physiological limits; further heating can erase local populations. This open-access Nature Communications article, corresponding-authored by Liang Ma, couples climate projections with biophysical models and species distributions to forecast physiological impacts on desert birds worldwide.

What the paper shows

Impacts are heterogeneous between and within warm deserts, and spatial patterns of physiological stress do not simply mirror air-temperature change. The authors define climate-change refugia as warm-desert areas that retain high avian diversity with comparatively low predicted physiological impact. Those refugia persist to varying extents across desert realms — yet only a small fraction (<20%) currently fall inside protected areas.

For APEC students the paper is both result and method lesson: microclimate modelling, validated avian physiology, and spatial analysis in one pipeline — built with collaborators spanning Princeton, South Africa and Australia.
