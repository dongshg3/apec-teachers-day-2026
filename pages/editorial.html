Editorial

The mentor as microclimate: buffering the next generation of ecologists

APEC Editorial · Teachers' Day Special

Published 7 September 2026 · Updated for 10 September delivery

In physiological ecology, microclimate is not a detail — it is the difference between a lethal surface and a survivable retreat. Shade, moisture, elevation and hour of day rewrite the temperatures an animal actually experiences. Mentorship works in much the same way. A good advisor does not remove the heat of science; they change the exposure.

This special issue is our Teachers' Day letter to 马亮 (Liang Ma): youth researcher and doctoral supervisor at the School of Ecology, Sun Yat-sen University; Assistant Dean; and principal investigator of the APEC Lab — Animal Physiological Ecology and Conservation. His programme asks how terrestrial vertebrates, especially reptiles and birds, absorb environmental change at each life stage, and how protection can be redesigned when maps of adult risk fail to cover embryos.

“真正有效的保护应覆盖生命的每个阶段。” — a line that also describes what teaching owes students: not only the visible adult career, but the fragile early stages.

Why a Nature-shaped issue

APEC publishes in the journals students grow up reading — Nature, Nature Communications, Global Change Biology, Conservation Biology, Current Biology. For Teachers' Day we borrow the cadence of a Nature issue: editorial, profile, research features, news & comment, and correspondence. The correspondence section is reserved for one sentence from each group member. The science pages reconnect those sentences to the papers that define the lab's public record.

What this issue contains

Features revisit two open-access Nature Communications articles that bookend APEC's global ambition: climate impacts on desert bird communities, and protected areas as refuges for amphibians and reptiles. News & Comment traces the embryo–adult mismatch that APEC has pressed into conservation planning. The reading list places those contributions beside related Nature portfolio work on energetics and thermal barriers — not as decoration, but as a curated shelf for the people Ma Liang trains.

Photographs of the mentor and the lab will appear when available. Until then, placeholders mark where reality will land — a small reminder that even polished journals begin as drafts.

Happy Teachers' Day, 马老师. May your students inherit both your questions and your patience with unfinished maps.

— The APEC Lab, Shenzhen Campus, Sun Yat-sen University
